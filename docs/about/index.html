Postdoctoral Fellow · Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine

Research at the intersection of neurology and proteomics.

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Neurology and Institute for Cell Engineering at Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine in Baltimore, Maryland.

My research focuses on mass spectrometry-based protein biomarker discovery in neurodegenerative disease, including quantitative proteomics of human cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and plasma. I work across sample preparation, LC-MS acquisition, targeted analysis, and computational interpretation in collaboration with clinician investigators.

Research focus

  • Neurodegenerative disease biomarkers and collaborative neurology research
  • CSF and plasma proteomics
  • Targeted mass spectrometry using PRM and stable isotope-labeled peptide standards
  • DIA proteomics and quantitative data analysis

Experimental and computational capabilities

My broader background includes LC-MS/MS analytical and bioanalytical method development, pharmacokinetic research, and Python/R-based analysis. Laboratory automation supports this work through repeatable plate-based workflows and custom Opentrons protocols.

The case studies explain the scientific or practical question, my specific contribution, the decisions behind the workflow, and the evidence supporting the result. They distinguish individual work from collaborative contributions and report performance within the conditions actually evaluated.
